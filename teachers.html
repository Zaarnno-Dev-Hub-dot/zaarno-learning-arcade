Learning Arcade: a guide for parents and teachers

Six free games for ages 6 to 12 that turn short practice into play: touch typing, math facts, spelling and geography. There are no accounts, ads or tracking. Each game runs in a web browser on a computer, tablet or phone. They are practice, not instruction, so they work best beside what your child is already learning.

What each game teaches

GameAges · gradesWhat it practicesHow it plays
Plumber Typing7+ · grades 2 and upTouch typing with the correct fingers: the home row first, then the top row, then the bottom row, then speed and longer words. Each level only uses keys already learned.Type the word on each clog before it reaches the plumber. An on-screen keyboard shows which finger to use. 12 levels, 1 to 3 stars each. A slower speed setting is available.
Needs a keyboard.
MathMan6+ · grades 1 and upAddition, subtraction, times tables and division facts. Easy stays within 10 (tables to 5), medium within 20 (tables to 10), hard uses two-digit numbers (tables to 12).A maze chase. Eating a glowing orb asks a multiple-choice problem. A wrong answer shows the right one.
Keyboard, swipe or on-screen pad.
Math Quest Runner6 to 11 · grades 1 to 5Grade 1: add and subtract within 20, missing numbers, patterns, coins. Grade 2: within 100, place value, first times tables, time. Grade 3: multiplication and division facts, rounding, area and perimeter. Grade 4: two-digit times one-digit, fractions, factors and measures. Grade 5: fractions with different bottoms, decimals, percent, averages, order of operations. 31 kinds of question, including word problems.A jungle platformer: touch a coin or a gremlin to get a question. After a wrong answer it explains why and shows the steps. Music, moving and crumbling platforms.
Keyboard or touch. Lives on its own website.
Math Blaster6+ · grades 1 to 5Quick arithmetic in seven modes: addition and subtraction (easy and medium), multiplication, division and a mixed mode, each at three difficulty levels. The wrong answers are close to the right one, so each block needs real thought.A problem shows at the top and answer blocks fall. Tap or shoot the right block, or type the answer.
Keyboard, mouse or touch.
Spelling Bee7+ · grades 2 to 8Spelling about 570 words in four difficulty tiers, from short everyday words to challenging vocabulary. Five modes: see it, hear it, definition clues, speed round, unscramble.A wrong answer shows the correct spelling before moving on. Words can be read aloud by the device (where it has a voice).
Keyboard, or the on-screen keyboard on touch screens.
GeoQuest8+ · grades 3 and upWhere countries, capitals and famous landmarks are on a world map, and which flag belongs to which country.Click or tap the answer on the map. Easy mode labels the map, hard mode does not.
Mouse or touch.

Using it well

Sessions

  • Ten focused minutes a few times a week beats one long session.
  • Let your child pick the game; pick the difficulty together. Start easy and move up when the score stops climbing slowly.
  • Sit with younger children the first time and talk through a missed problem.

Progress

  • The My Progress page shows stars and best scores for every game in one place.
  • Progress is saved in this browser on this device only. A different browser or device starts fresh.
  • Math Quest Runner keeps its own record on its own website.

Privacy and access

  • No sign-up, no ads, no analytics, no third-party scripts. Nothing is sent anywhere.
  • Every game has a sound button. Most work on a phone held sideways.
  • The code is open source (MIT), so a school can host its own copy.
